# Southeast Asia PCA groups

The groups combine language families, geography, population history, and G25 affinity. They are reference clusters, so a language label does not determine every assignment.

| Group | References and rationale |
| --- | --- |
| Tai-Kadai | Thai, Lao, Tai Lue, Dai, Bouyei, and Zhuang. Main includes Chinese Bouyei/Zhuang anchors as well as Thai/Lao/Dai. Vietnamese (Dong Profile) belongs here by profile: its closest global reference is Dong Hunan, while the Kinh-profile Vietnamese references stay Austroasiatic. |
| Hmong-Mien | Hmong, Miao, and Bunu. Hmong Thailand, Miao Leishan, and Bunu Youmai are Main anchors; regional Miao/Bunu references extend Deep. All added rows come from the existing global modern dataset. |
| Austroasiatic | Mainland Mon-Khmer, Vietic, Palaungic, and related references. Nyah Kur belongs here. Nicobarese keeps its existing Deep Mixed status with the corrected group assignment. |
| Tibeto-Burman | Burmese, Rakhine, Karen, Loloish, Chin-Kuki-Mizo, Naga, Nyishi, Garo, Tripuri, and the Magar/Tamang branch. Hani belongs here; Hmong belongs in Hmong-Mien. |
| Indo-Burmese | Assamese, Rohingya, Tharu, Rajbanshi, Manipuri Brahmin, Khasi, Kusunda, and Tibetan Muslim Srinagar. This is a geographic/genetic borderland cluster, not a language family: Khasi is Austroasiatic-speaking and Kusunda speaks an isolate. Their whole-profile affinities guide these assignments. |
| Malay Negrito | Batek, Jehai, Kintaq, Maniq, and Mendriq. This separates the Semang-related references from Malay/Senoi and Philippine populations. |
| Philippine Negrito | Aeta, Agta, Mamanwa, and Batak Palawan. Batak Palawan is distinct from Batak Toba in Western Austronesian. |
| Malay Peninsula | Peninsular Malay/Orang Asli Austronesian and Senoi references, including Jakun, Temuan, Seletar, Temiar, Cheq Wong, Mah Meri, and coastal Moken. A regional label accommodates their different languages and shared local admixture histories. |
| Western Austronesian | Most western Indonesian and Bornean references, plus nearby maritime populations with similar profiles. |
| Taiwan-Philippines | Indigenous Taiwanese and non-Negrito Philippine Austronesian references. Existing Han-mixed rows retain their Deep status. |
| Wallacea | Eastern Indonesian/Timorese references with varying Austronesian and Papuan-related backgrounds. Ngadha Bena belongs here alongside other Flores populations. The regional name accommodates both Austronesian and non-Austronesian languages. |

Malay and Philippine Negrito references are separate because their population histories and G25 profiles differ. The mean between-region G25 distance in this dataset is about 0.111, compared with about 0.030 within the Malay set and 0.039 within the Philippine set. These distances are in scaled G25 units, before the app's display multiplier.

Neolithic proxies inform the interpretation of farmer-related and older hunter-gatherer backgrounds. They do not override whole-profile affinity or justify treating a fitted component as measured ancestry. In particular, the low Southeast Asian farmer-proxy weight of some Naga/Nyishi references does not exclude them from the regional Tibeto-Burman group.

## Boundary with the Tibet group

Kusunda and Tibetan Muslim Srinagar fit the Indo-Burmese references better than the core Tibetan/Sherpa references. Tamang and Tamang Simigaon fit Tibeto-Burman better. Magar is borderline by fit and stays with the nearby Tamang branch. Main/Deep status is preserved: Simigaon is a Deep addition.

Tibetan Nepal, the plateau Tibetan references, Sherpa, Gurung, and Tamang Tashinam retain stronger plateau affinities and remain in East Asia's Tibet group. Population-specific assignments matter: the Tamang profiles do not all follow the same branch.
